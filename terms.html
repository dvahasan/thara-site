What Thara is

Thara is a personal finance application for Android. It keeps your records in an encrypted database on your phone and a sealed copy in your Thara account. The same app can also be opened in a web browser: there the working copy of your records is kept in the browser's storage instead, as the privacy policy describes. It is provided free of charge, without any subscription or in-app purchase. By installing or using it you agree to these terms.

Your Thara account

Thara needs a free Thara account, made with an email address or with a Google account. Use an email address that is yours, keep your password and your PIN to yourself, and tell the developer if you think someone else has signed in to your account. An account is for one person's own finances; family accounts are how you share a source with others. You can close your account at any time from the app: that deletes it and everything kept with it, as the privacy policy describes.

No advice

Everything Thara shows is calculated from figures you entered and rates you recorded or fetched. Plans, projections, grades and verdicts are arithmetic on those inputs. They are not investment, financial, tax or legal advice, and nothing in the app promises any return. Projections use the assumptions you set, and they are labelled as projections.

Answers from Thara AI, and what it reads from a receipt, a bank message or a sentence you say, are written by AI and can be wrong. Check a record before you save it, and treat an answer as general information, not advice.

The zakat plan and calculator are arithmetic, not a religious ruling. Which assets count, which debts may be deducted and when a holding year completes vary by school and by circumstance. Consult a qualified scholar for a ruling on your own situation.

Your data and your backups

Your records live on your phone, in your Thara account and in any backups you choose to make. Losing or replacing the phone does not lose them: sign in on the new one and they come back. Closing your account deletes the copy kept in it, and that cannot be undone. Anything recorded on a phone that has not yet reached your account is lost if the app is deleted first; in a browser, the same goes for clearing the browser's data for the site. Backups made by older builds with a passphrase or a recovery code open only with that passphrase or code, and nobody, the developer included, can open them without it.
