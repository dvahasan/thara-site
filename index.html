Everything a finance app should do, without the cloud

Thara is a complete personal finance manager. It was built for people who want the full picture of their money and do not want to hand that picture to anyone.

Sources المصادر

Cash, bank accounts, mobile wallets, cards, savings, investments, money owed to you and debts. Each in its own currency, with balances computed from your records rather than stored and drifted.

Records السجلات

Expenses, income and transfers between your own sources. Transfers are tracked but kept out of spending analysis, because moving money between your pockets is not spending it.

Types الأنواع

Sort spending however you like. Each type has an icon, a colour, an optional monthly limit and a needs, wants or saving class. About 35 Arabic and English types are ready on first run.

Obligations الالتزامات

Rent, instalments, utilities, loans, subscriptions and tax, each with its due date and repeat rule. Paying one writes the record for you. Standing orders log themselves once their date has passed.

Analysis التحليل

Monthly and yearly views: income, spending, savings rate, where it went by type, needs, wants and saving split, biggest changes, and net worth over the last twelve months.

Looking ahead التخطيط

Pick two dates and Thara walks the months between them: expected income, ordinary costs, what falls due and where net worth ends up. Months that cannot pay for themselves are called out by name.

Dollar and gold الدولار والذهب

Pick your market, record what a dollar and a gram actually cost, or fetch the published rates once a day. A rate you typed is never overwritten by a downloaded one.

Gold you hold ذهبك

Physical metal by weight and karat, not as a balance you keep rewriting. Thara shows what it is worth if sold and what buying it again would cost. The gap is the workmanship you paid.

Real wealth قيمة ثروتك

Net worth month by month in your currency, in grams of pure gold and in dollars, each month at the rate that held then. A balance up thirty per cent has not grown if it buys less gold.

Zakat الزكاة

Arithmetic, not a ruling. 2.5% of qualifying wealth against a gold or silver nisab over a 354-day lunar year. The plan tracks your holding year; the calculator works from figures you type.

Reminders التنبيهات

Off until you switch them on. A bill falling due, one already overdue, a zakat year completing, a spending cap passed, or a backup that has not run in a long time. Each one separately.

Arabic and English عربي وإنجليزي

Full right-to-left layout, optional Arabic-Indic numerals, and a first-run guide you can reopen from Settings at any topic. Try it with two years of sample data before entering anything.

Nine plans, graded against your real records

Run as many as you like at once. Each produces a status, a progress bar, the numbers behind it and specific advice. Projections are labelled as projections and use the return you assume, never one the app promises.

Built like a vault, because it holds your money

This is the part that matters for a finance app, so the site is specific about it.

Encrypted at rest

All data sits in a SQLCipher AES-256 database. The key is 256 random bits generated on the device at first run and held in the Android Keystore or iOS Keychain, hardware-backed on most modern phones. Copying the database file off the phone yields ciphertext.

App lock

Optional PIN, hashed with PBKDF2 and a random salt, with fingerprint or face unlock on top. Wrong attempts trigger an escalating cooldown. The PIN is deliberately not the encryption key, so a short PIN cannot weaken the database.

Nothing on screen leaks

Screenshots, screen recording and the recent-apps thumbnail are blocked. A hide-amounts toggle replaces every figure with dots. Auto-lock is configurable from immediately to never.

Two network calls, both switchable

Rate updates send only the currency being asked about. The update check asks this site once a day whether a newer build exists, carries no identifier and sends nothing about your wallet. With both switched off, the app makes no requests at all. There is no analytics and no crash reporting.

Backups that only you can read

Thara backs up into a folder you choose, on a schedule, or to a file you save or share anywhere. Every backup is encrypted on the phone first with a passphrase you choose. Without it the file cannot be opened, by the developer or by anyone who finds it.

Thara writes the file and nothing else. Whether that folder is on the phone, on an SD card, or inside a cloud app you already have is decided by you and by whatever app owns it — not by Thara, which never talks to any of them. Google Drive backup is not offered at present.

  1. 1
    Choose a passphraseAt least 12 characters. It is never stored in the backup and never leaves the phone.
  2. 2
    Encrypt on the deviceAES-256-GCM with a key derived by PBKDF2 at 600,000 rounds and a fresh salt every time.
  3. 3
    Write the ciphertextInto the folder you picked, on the phone or on an SD card. Older automatic copies are pruned so it never fills up.
  4. 4
    Restore anywhereEnter the same passphrase on another phone. Restore runs in one transaction, so an interrupted restore leaves the old wallet intact.

Each link always serves the newest build: every run replaces the files on the same release, so the address never changes and never goes stale. Both platforms share the same encrypted backup format, so a backup made on one restores on the other.

You only have to find this page once. Because Thara is not in a store, it keeps track for itself: once a day it reads version.json on this site, and when a newer build appears it tells you — in the app's own notification list, as a notification from the phone, and as a prompt that shows up once and then leaves you alone for several days. The check carries no identifier and sends nothing about your wallet, and it can be switched off under Settings, About.

Installing it

Thara is not in either app store, so the two platforms are not the same amount of work. Android is one tap on the phone. iPhone needs a computer once, because Apple only runs apps carrying a signature it trusts, and only a computer can ask Apple for one.

Android — on the phone Ready to use

  1. 1
    Open this page on the phoneThen press Download APK above.
  2. 2
    Allow the installAndroid asks whether your browser may install apps. Say yes; it is asking because the file did not come from the Play Store.
  3. 3
    Open itThat is all. Later builds install over the top and keep everything in your wallet.

iPhone — a computer once Testing only

Both routes below sign the app with your own Apple ID. Nothing is sent to the developer, and the signature is what lets your phone trust an app that did not come from the App Store. On a free Apple ID a signature lasts seven days; renewing it does not touch your data.

  1. 1
    SideStore — the computer is needed onceSet it up from a Windows PC or a Mac following sidestore.io. After that the phone renews the app by itself over Wi-Fi, with no cable and no computer ever again. This is the one to pick if somebody else will be living with it.
  2. 2
    Sideloadly — simpler, but every weekInstall iTunes from Apple (the download from Apple's own site, not the Microsoft Store version, which leaves out the device drivers) and Sideloadly. Plug the phone in, drag the IPA onto the window, enter your Apple ID, press Start. Quicker to set up; needs the cable again every seven days.
  3. 3
    Trust it on the phoneEither way, the first launch is refused until you go to Settings → General → VPN & Device Management, tap your Apple ID and choose Trust.

Two Apple limits worth knowing before you start: a free Apple ID may hold three sideloaded apps at once, and if your Apple ID uses two-factor authentication you will need an app-specific password rather than your real one.

Before you reinstall anything, take a backup

Renewing a signature keeps your wallet exactly as it is. Deleting the app does not — the encrypted database goes with it, and nobody can bring it back, including the developer. If Thara is already on that phone, open Settings → Save an encrypted backup first and put the file somewhere that is not the phone. On iPhone you can fetch backups afterwards from Files → On My iPhone → Thara → Thara Backups.
