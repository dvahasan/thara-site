Thara Privacy

Privacy policy

Last updated 15 November 2026. Thara in a browser (the web version): where a browser keeps your wallet, whom the page talks to, and what it leaves out. The site's own feedback form, in place of the Google Form; download counts, once per browser; the Thara survey. Thara 2.2.2: a forgotten PIN reset through your account; live talk that opens the app for you, looks things up and proposes changes; exactly what Thara AI can look up. Earlier in 2.2: Thara accounts and the sealed wallet; Thara AI and its two modes; bank messages read on the phone; family accounts; emailed reports; backups locked with your account.

The short version. Thara keeps your financial records in an encrypted database on your phone, and a sealed copy in your Thara account, so a new phone signs in and has them back (Your Thara account). Thara's service keeps the key to that copy: that is how a reset by email or a Google sign-in can always bring your wallet back. Thara AI works on the phone where it can. What it sends online depends on the AI mode you choose, and Thara asks before the first time. A question that goes online carries a short profile with your sources' balances. In Enhanced AI mode, Thara AI can also look up the part of your wallet a question needs, including records with their notes and shops (Thara AI). The text of your bank messages is read on the phone and never sent (Bank messages). Your PIN never leaves the phone (The app lock). Reports of errors and freezes go to the developer, with nothing of your records in them (Problem reports). The app has no advertising and no analytics; this website counts each browser that downloads it once, and keeps the answers to its survey (Download counts, The survey). Thara can also be opened in a web browser: there the working copy of your wallet is kept in the browser's storage, without the phone's encryption (Thara in a browser).

What Thara stores

Thara stores the sources, records, types, obligations, plans, goals, gold holdings, shopping lists, exchange rates and settings you enter, the facts you ask it to remember, and the recent conversation with Talk to Thara. All of it lives in a SQLCipher encrypted database on your device. The encryption key is generated on the device and kept in the Android Keystore. The same wallet, sealed as one encrypted file, is kept in your Thara account, described next.

Your Thara account

Thara needs a free Thara account, made with an email address and a password, or with a Google account. When you make one with an email, Thara sends a six-digit code to that address to confirm it is yours; the same kind of code lets you set a new password if you forget it. Codes are good for fifteen minutes and are kept only as a scrambled code (a hash).

What the account keeps. Your email address; your name, if you give one; your language; a value worked out from your password on the phone, scrambled again before it is kept, so your password itself never leaves the phone; for an account made with Google, the identifier of that Google account; when the account was made and last changed; your sign-ins (each kept only as a scrambled code, for up to a year); the random number of the phone that last wrote your wallet (see Thara's gateway); your backups kept in the account; and how many emailed reports went out that day. It keeps no network addresses.

The sealed wallet. Your wallet is encrypted on the phone before it leaves it, and kept in the account as one sealed file, in a database run by MongoDB Atlas for Thara's service. The latest copy and the three before it are kept; older ones are deleted. Only one phone at a time can change the wallet; another phone signed in to the same account shows it read-only until you choose to move to it. While a phone is offline it keeps working on its own copy and sends the changes when it is back online.

The key to it. So that a reset by email, or signing in with Google, can always bring your wallet back, the phone gives Thara's service a copy of your wallet's key when the account is made. The service keeps that copy locked with a master key that lives only in its own storage at Cloudflare: never in the database, never in a log. It unlocks it only to hand it to a phone that has just proved it is you, with Google or an emailed code. Thara's code never opens your wallet on the service's side. But because the service holds the key, it could: your wallet is not sealed the way a passphrase only you know would seal it. The database on its own cannot open it.

Closing your account. You can close your account from your profile in the app. That deletes the account, every copy of the sealed wallet, the backups kept in the account, the key, your sign-ins and any codes waiting. It does not reach things kept elsewhere: backups you saved to a folder or to your Google Drive stay until you delete them, records you wrote into a family account stay in that family account, and emails already sent stay in your mailbox.

Thara in a browser

Thara can also be opened as a page in a web browser, at dvahasan.github.io/thara-web. It is the same app, signed in to the same Thara account, so your phone and the browser show one wallet, and only one of them can change it at a time. This section says how the web version differs from the phone app that the rest of this policy describes.

Where your wallet is kept. The web version keeps a working copy of your wallet in the browser's own storage for the site. That copy is not encrypted the way the phone's database is. What protects it is the browser keeping each site's storage apart from every other site's, your own sign-in to the computer, and Thara's PIN. The PIN stops someone who opens the page from seeing your wallet, but it does not encrypt the stored copy: anyone who can use the same browser profile on that computer could get at it. The receipt pictures you keep, your PIN's scrambled value and the keys a phone would hold in its keystore are kept in that same storage. The web version is served from the same web address as this website (dvahasan.github.io), so the browser counts the two as one site.

On a shared computer, sign out of Thara when you finish. Signing out first makes sure your account has everything, then removes the wallet from that browser: its records, sources, settings and lock. Clearing the browser's data for the site also removes the copy from that browser, together with anything that had not yet reached your account; it does not touch the copy in your account.

The lasting copy is the one in your Thara account, as described above: your wallet is sealed with the account's key in the browser, before it leaves it, and kept in the account as one sealed file. It is the same account, with the same key kept by Thara's service and the same reset by email.

What the page loads, and whom it talks to. The page loads no script, font or picture from any other site: everything it runs and shows comes from its own address, and it tells the browser to refuse anything else. Its files are served by GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, sees the network address that asks for them. Beyond its own address, the page can talk only to Thara's gateway; to the AI service's voice connection during a live talk, with the pass good for one talk that the gateway gives it; and to the public lists of exchange rates that the phone app reads too, when rates are updated. It makes no update check: the browser opens the version that is published, and reloading the page brings in a newer one. As published, the web version sends no problem reports to the error-tracking service.

Thara AI in a browser works in the same two modes, and receives what it receives from the phone (see Thara AI), with two differences. A browser has no text reader of its own, so a receipt picture is read only by Thara AI online, in Enhanced AI mode; in Private mode, or before you have allowed Thara AI online, the picture is not sent and you enter the record yourself. And a spoken record that Thara AI does not write down can only be written down by the browser's own speech recognition, which sends your voice to the company that makes the browser; Thara uses it only if you switch on Use online recognition.

What the web version does not have:

  • Reading bank messages: a browser has no text messages.
  • Home-screen widgets.
  • Fingerprint or face unlock: the PIN is its lock.
  • Phone notifications. Reminders and notices are listed under the bell inside Thara while the page is open; the browser shows none outside Thara, and nothing arrives while the page is closed. News from a family account arrives while Thara is open.
  • Automatic backups into a folder, and Google Drive backup.
  • The on-phone text reader, as said above.
  • “Continue with Google”, which is not offered in a browser yet: you sign in with your email and password. An account made with Google gets a password through Forgot password?, with a code sent to its email address; like any reset by email, that signs your other devices out.

The microphone and the camera are asked for by the browser itself, the first time you use them.

Backup files. In a browser you download a backup file instead. It is the same encrypted file the phone app makes, locked with your Thara account (see How backups are protected), and the phone app and the web version each open the other's. The browser saves it with its other downloads, outside Thara.

Network access

Thara uses the internet only for the features below:

  • Your Thara account. Signing in, and keeping the sealed wallet up to date, through Thara's gateway.
  • Thara AI. Only in the ways it asks you about first; see below.
  • Family accounts. Only if you make or join one, with notifications through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging; see below.
  • Emailed reports. Only if you ask for them; see below.
  • Rate updates. Off until you switch them on, or press to update the rates yourself. Fetches one public file of published exchange rates and the world gold price, the same file for everyone, from a free currency service served through jsDelivr or Cloudflare Pages. The request carries no balance, no identifier and no account; the phone works out your own currency and gold prices from it.
  • The update check. On by default, because Thara is installed as a file rather than from a store and nothing else would ever tell you that a fix has shipped. When a new build is first seen it is announced in the app's own notification list, and once as a phone notification if you have switched notifications on and kept A new version of Thara on; Thara never asks for notification permission for this. Once a day the app fetches version.json from this website and compares the build number in it with its own. The request is a plain GET for a public file: it carries no identifier, no account and nothing about your wallet, and the reply is the same bytes for everyone. Thara follows a download link only to this site or its releases, shows the fingerprints of the file and of the certificate that signed it, and treats an update as required only when the file is signed with the developer's key. You can switch it off under Settings, About.
  • Google Drive backup. Only if you connect a Google account to it; see below.
  • Problem reports. Errors and freezes, sent to the developer; see below.
  • The phone's own speech recognition. Off unless you switch on Use online recognition: then your phone's speech service, not Thara, may send the sound of a spoken record to its maker, under that maker's terms.

Thara contains no advertising and no analytics.

Thara's gateway. Apart from the update check, rate updates, Google Drive, problem reports and the sound of a live talk, everything Thara sends goes to a small service the developer runs on Cloudflare. It signs you in, keeps the sealed wallet in the database, keeps family accounts in step, sends Thara's mail, and passes Thara AI's requests on to the AI service under the developer's key, which the app does not carry. It does not keep or log what is sent to the AI service or what it answers. So that no one phone or network can use up everyone's share, each copy of Thara sends a random number made when it was installed, together with its build number; the gateway counts each day's requests per install, per network address (kept only as a scrambled code, never the address) and in all, and deletes those counts after two days. The same random number is kept with your account as the phone that last wrote your wallet.

Thara AI

Thara AI is the assistant and the reader inside Thara. Some of it runs on the phone itself; the rest runs online, on the computers of an outside AI service, reached through Thara's gateway. When you first open Thara, it asks once whether Thara AI may work online, saying what would be sent. Until you allow it, nothing is sent to the AI service. If you allow it, Enhanced AI mode is turned on; if you choose to keep everything on the phone, Private mode is. You can change modes at any time under Settings, AI permissions and privacy:

  • Private mode. Your records, calculations, plans, search and reminders are never sent to an AI. Receipts and spoken records are read on the phone only, and live talk is off. If you have allowed Thara AI online at some point, a general question that is not about your wallet (“are bank certificates worth it?”) is still answered online; if you never allowed it, such questions are not answered.
  • Enhanced AI mode. Then these are sent as well: every receipt photo you scan, to be read beside the phone's own reading; a record you say or type, as the sound of what you say, to write it down, and as the sentence, with the names of your sources and types; what Thara AI looks up in the app to answer you or to prepare a change you asked for (see What Thara AI can look up); and, if you switch on live talk, your voice.

What Thara AI can look up. In Enhanced AI mode, in Talk to Thara and in a live talk, Thara AI answers many questions by asking the phone for one part of your wallet. The phone checks your permissions, then sends back only that part. Thara AI can ask for:

  • your records one by one, up to 100 at a time, each with its date, amount, source, type, shop and note;
  • your sources with their balances, and your types with their monthly caps;
  • plans with their lines, goals and shopping lists;
  • monthly income, obligations and gameyas;
  • zakat and gold;
  • bank messages waiting to be added, as the date, amount and shop worked out from each (never the message itself);
  • the corrections Thara learnt from your receipts;
  • the facts you asked it to remember;
  • your currency, language, appearance and name;
  • which notifications are on, and what is due soon;
  • for a family account you belong to: its shared sources and balances, and its members' names with what each has left to spend this month.

Switching off View financial records under Settings, AI permissions and privacy, stops all of these look-ups. In Private mode none of them are sent.

The short profile. In either mode, a question that goes online carries a short profile, so the answer fits you: your name, your currency, your sources with their balances, and the names of your types, together with up to six earlier lines of the same conversation so that a follow-up makes sense.

In either mode, your backups and your PIN are never sent to Thara AI, and most answers about your wallet are worked out on the phone. A receipt photo is drawn again before it is sent, which leaves behind what the camera wrote into the file (where and when it was taken, and on which phone). A general question is answered with web search allowed, so the answer can use current facts, and the sites it leaned on are shown under it. Answers are labelled as written by AI: they may be wrong, and they are general information, not advice. Under its terms, the AI service may use what is sent with a free-of-charge key to improve its products; a paid key stops that.

Talking live. Only in Enhanced AI mode, only if you switch on Talk live, and only after Thara asks once. Thara's gateway gives the phone a pass good for one talk, and the phone then streams the sound of your voice straight to the AI service, which hears it, answers aloud, and writes down what both of you said; that text appears in the conversation.

  • Looking things up. During a talk, Thara AI can look things up as described under What Thara AI can look up, and the phone sends back what it asked for.
  • Using the app. Thara AI can open a part of the app for you, go back or home, and explain a screen. The explanation comes from Thara's own guide on the phone; nothing on your screen is captured or sent. When Thara AI opens a screen, the talk shrinks to a floating Thara circle and goes on while you use the app.
  • Changes. Thara AI can only propose: a record, a plan, or a change to something already saved, including deleting it. Each is shown to you first, as a draft or a card, and is made only when you press Save or Apply, or agree aloud.
  • Remembering. If you ask it to remember something, it keeps that as a fact (see The conversation).
  • Limits and the microphone. A talk lasts up to ten minutes, and a few talks a day are included. The microphone stays on while the talk or its floating circle is showing; you can mute it from the circle. It stops when you press Stop, when you end the talk from the circle, or when the ten minutes are up.

The conversation. The recent conversation with Talk to Thara is kept in the wallet's encrypted database, so a follow-up makes sense; New chat clears it. What you ask Thara to remember is kept the same way, and can be changed or deleted under Settings, Memory.

Thara AI online and live talk are for adults aged 18 and over; Thara says so before they are first used.

Reading a receipt

When you scan a receipt to fill in a record, the app's own camera view takes one picture and reads it on your phone, with the text recognition built into the app and, on phones that carry an AI model of their own, with that model, on the phone. In Enhanced AI mode the photo is also sent to Thara AI online, to be read at the same time; in Private mode it never is. The lines of the receipt you accept are kept with the record, in the same encrypted database as everything else. When you correct a reading — move the total, fix a date, name an item, file a card slip under an account, choose a different type, turn a statement row between money in and money out — the app remembers that correction for the same shop, in the same encrypted database, so the next receipt from there is read right. These lessons are not part of a backup file, and can be forgotten at any time from Settings. They stay on the phone, except that in Enhanced AI mode Thara AI can look them up: the shop and what was corrected (see What Thara AI can look up).

When you save a scanned record, the app keeps one copy of the picture, encrypted, in its own storage. A receipt shows where you were and what you bought, and a bank statement shows a month of your life, so it is sealed with the same kind of encryption as your ledger, under a separate key held by your phone's keystore. No other app can read it, and the app itself only unseals it into memory while you are looking at it — open the record and tap the picture tile.

A copy in your photo gallery happens only when you ask for one. In the picture viewer, "Save a copy to the gallery" puts one in an album called Thara. That copy is yours rather than the app's: it is not encrypted, it is not covered by the app's lock, it follows whatever your phone does with photo backups, and any app you have given access to your photos can see it. The app tells you so when it makes one, and never deletes a gallery copy afterwards — delete it like any other photo.

Deleting a record deletes the app's copy of its picture, unless another record still uses it: the rows of one bank statement share a picture, so it survives until the last of them is deleted. Erasing everything erases the pictures too. Pictures are not in a backup file or in the sealed wallet, so a record restored on another phone keeps what the receipt said but shows its picture as missing.

Speaking a record

In Private mode, what you say into the microphone is written down on the phone, by the phone's own speech recognition. In Enhanced AI mode, the sound is sent to Thara AI to be written down (the phone does it instead if that fails), and the sentence, with the names of your sources and types, to be turned into a record. The recording is not kept. Nothing is saved until you check the record and save it; by voice, a record is saved only when you say a word of saving (“save it”, «احفظه»), a plain “yes” is not enough, and any “no” cancels.

Bank messages

On Android, if you turn on Bank messages, Thara asks for permission to read your text messages and to be woken when one arrives. It then reads the messages that come from named senders, such as your banks, wallets, InstaPay and card companies, on the phone; works out which are about money spent or received; and turns each into a record for the right source, either by itself or for you to approve, as you choose. When you first turn it on, it also offers the money messages of the last 90 days for you to add. It keeps reading in the background when Thara is closed, which you can switch off. Messages from a phone number are dropped before Thara reads a word of them, and codes, offers and personal messages are left alone. The messages, and what Thara learns from them (which source a sender's messages belong to), stay on the phone: no message is sent to Thara's gateway, to Thara AI or anywhere else. In Enhanced AI mode, Thara AI can see the records waiting to be added from them, as their date, amount and shop only (see What Thara AI can look up). A record made from a message is an ordinary record: it goes into your wallet, its sealed copy and your backups, and into a family account if its source is shared. You can switch reading off at any time on the same screen, and withdraw the permission in Android's settings.

Family accounts

A family account lets people you invite record into sources you share. It needs a Thara account, and it is kept in step through Thara's gateway. Every record written into a shared source — its amount, type, note, shop and who wrote it — is sealed on the phone with the family's own key, which only the members' phones hold, before it leaves the phone. The gateway keeps those sealed records in order, a scrambled code of the family's token, and, for each member's phone, the address Google gives it for notifications and its language; it cannot read a record. Your own sources, and records you did not share, are never part of it. On your own phone, in Enhanced AI mode, Thara AI can look up a family account you belong to: its shared sources, their balances and its members' names (see What Thara AI can look up).

When someone records, the other members' phones are told through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging. The notification's own text is one of a few fixed labels, with no name and no amount, and the message also carries the sealed record, which Google cannot open. The member's phone opens it and shows who wrote what, and its amount.

Emailed reports

Off unless you turn them on in your profile. Then Thara emails you a report of your balances, weekly or monthly, or once when you ask: your net worth, the month's income, spending and what is left, each source's balance, the dues coming up and your goals' progress, each part of which you can leave out. The phone works the figures out; Thara's gateway lays them out in a fixed letter and sends it, through a mail service (Brevo or Resend), only to your account's confirmed email address, and keeps none of it, only a count of how many went out that day. An email is not sealed the way your wallet is: it can be read by whoever can read your mailbox. You can stop the reports at any time on the same screen. The same mail service sends the codes that confirm your address and reset your password.

Notifications

Notifications are off until you switch them on, and each kind has its own switch: bills coming up, late or not covered by their source; going over a type's monthly cap; a zakat year completing; goals at risk, time to put money aside for one, and paused goals to resume; payday; coaching when a type of spending runs well ahead of your usual month; a weekly review on the day you choose; records that need a look; shopping-list reminders; new bank messages; an evening reminder on a day nothing was recorded (off unless you turn it on); backups; family accounts; and new versions. Apart from family accounts, all of them are worked out on the phone, from your own records, by the app's own calculations: nothing is sent anywhere to decide them and no AI writes them.

A notification can be read on a locked screen by whoever holds the phone, so a notification never shows a balance. It does name what happened, with its amount — which bill, which shop, how much — unless you switch off Details in notifications; then it says only “A bill is due in 3 days”, and the list inside Thara still has the details. A family account's notification shows who wrote what, with its amount, whichever way that switch is set. Nothing that can wait arrives between 10 pm and 8 am, at most two such notifications come on one day (the rest are gathered into one), and each comes once. Whether notifications are on, and the details switch, belong to the phone: a wallet restored onto another phone starts with them off until that phone is asked.

Problem reports

So that crashes and freezes can be found and fixed, Thara records what went wrong: the error, where in the code it happened, the screen it was on (by the screen's name in the code, not anything shown on it), how long a freeze lasted, the phone's model and operating-system version, and Thara's version. These are kept on the phone, listed under Settings, Problems, where you can read them, send them to the developer yourself, or clear them.

They are also sent automatically to the developer's error-tracking service, Sentry, unless you switch that off on the same screen. A report carries the kind of error and where in Thara's code it happened, never the error's own message, so nothing the app was handling at the time — an amount, a source or shop name, a sentence — is in it. No screenshot, no text you typed, no account and no device name is included. Like any service on the internet, Sentry sees the network address a report comes from. Problem reports are used only to fix the app.

Links the app can open

Settings carries a few links: the feedback form, the download page, and this policy. Tapping one hands the address to whatever your phone uses for links and that is the end of Thara's part in it — the app fetches nothing, sends nothing, and attaches nothing about your wallet to the address. The feedback link carries the app's build number after a “#”, a part of an address that the browser never sends anywhere: it only fills in the form's build field, which you can clear before sending.

Feedback

The feedback form on this site sends what you write to Thara's gateway, which keeps it in Thara's own database (MongoDB Atlas) for the developer to read: the text, the kind you picked, the language it is written in, when it arrived, and, only if you fill them in, an email address to answer you at, the Thara build and the phone. Nothing else about you is kept with it: no name, no account and no network address. It needs no sign-in.

So that the form cannot be flooded, your browser keeps a random number made for this site, in its own storage, and sends it with each message; the gateway counts a day's messages by that number and by your network address (kept only as a scrambled code, never the address), and deletes those counts after two days, as it does for the app. The developer reads the messages on a private page that only their own Thara account can open, and keeps each one until it has been dealt with and deleted. To have yours deleted sooner, send another message asking for it, saying roughly when and what you wrote. Please leave real amounts, balances and account numbers out: knowing what happened is enough.

Download counts

So that the developer knows how many people have downloaded Thara, the first time a browser presses one of this site's download buttons it tells Thara's gateway, which keeps one record for that browser: a scrambled code (a SHA-256 hash) of the random number the site made for it, which file it chose, and when. Pressing again, then or later, sends nothing and changes nothing, so each browser is counted once. No network address, name or anything else is kept with it, and the code cannot be turned back into the random number. Another browser, a private window or cleared site data counts as a new browser. The developer sees only the totals.

The survey

The Thara survey asks ten questions; three are needed and the rest, including the written ones, are up to you. Your answers are kept in Thara's database under the same kind of scrambled code of your browser's random number, with when they were given, so that one browser has one answer: answering again replaces it. Nothing else about you is kept, and no sign-in or name is asked. The developer reads the totals and the written answers on their private page. Please leave real amounts and account numbers out of what you write.

How backups are protected

Besides the sealed wallet in your account, Thara can write backups to a folder you choose, to your Google Drive or to your Thara account, on a schedule (backups to the account are off unless you turn them on). Every backup is encrypted on the phone with AES-256-GCM. While you are signed in, the key comes from your wallet's key, so any phone signed in to the account can open it: there is no passphrase to choose and no recovery code to write down. Because Thara's service holds your wallet's key (see above), it could work this key out too. Backups made by earlier builds of Thara, locked with a passphrase or a recovery code, still open with that passphrase or code, and with nothing else. You can see and delete every backup, and choose how many automatic backups are kept, under Manage backups.

Google Drive backup

Drive backup is optional and off until you connect a Google account to it under Settings, Backup. Once connected, an encrypted backup is uploaded once a day, which one switch turns off; when Thara is set up on a new phone, it can find the backup in the same Google account and open it once you are signed in to your Thara account.

For Drive, Thara asks for a single permission: access to its own application data folder on your Google Drive. This is the drive.appdata scope. That folder is hidden from the regular Drive interface and is readable only by Thara. Thara cannot see, read, list or change any other file on your Drive. Because each backup is encrypted before it is uploaded, Google cannot read it.

You can disconnect the Google account from the app's Settings at any time. You can also revoke Thara's access from your Google account's third-party access page, and delete the backup from Google Drive under Settings, Manage apps.

Signing in with Google

If you make or open your Thara account with Google, Google tells Thara's service which Google account you used (its identifier), its email address and its name. The identifier is kept with your account so that Google can sign you in again; all three are used only to sign you in and to show you which account is signed in.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Thara's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data obtained through Google APIs is used only to sign you in to your Thara account and to store and retrieve your own encrypted backups, is never transferred to anyone else, and is never used for advertising.

Other backups and exports

You can also share an encrypted backup to another app. Files handed to the system save dialog go wherever you point them; Thara does not learn the destination and keeps no copy. Plain JSON and CSV exports are deliberately unencrypted, so treat them as you would any file containing your finances.

The app lock

Thara opens with a PIN of at least four digits, and with fingerprint or face unlock if you turn it on. The PIN is kept on the phone only as a scrambled value (a deliberately slow hash, held in the phone's keystore). It is never sent anywhere, not even to Thara's service.

If you forget your PIN on a phone signed in to your Thara account, you can prove it is you in one of three ways: with your account password, with Google, or with a code emailed to your account's address (which sets a new account password too). Then you choose a new PIN, and nothing on the phone is erased. Thara's service checks that proof the way it checks a sign-in; neither the old PIN nor the new one leaves the phone.

Anyone who can use your Google account or read your email on the phone could do the same, so keep the phone's own screen lock on.

Device permissions

  • Biometrics. Only if you enable fingerprint or face unlock. The app receives a yes or no from the operating system and never sees biometric data.
  • Notifications. Asked for only when you switch notifications on under Settings, Notifications, never at first launch. See Notifications.
  • Text messages. Asked for only when you turn on Bank messages, or grant it yourself from Thara's list of permissions in Settings. See Bank messages.
  • Storage. Folder backups use the system document picker with a permission on one folder. No general storage permission is requested.
  • Microphone. Asked for the first time you press the microphone, and used only while you hold or lock the record button or a live talk is on.
  • Camera. Asked for the first time you press Scan, and used only while that scanner view is open. Thara never opens the camera on its own.
